Pass rates

Manchester driving test pass rates, explained with real odds

DVSA figures show a 21-point gap between Bolton and Bury. We turned pass rates into the numbers that matter: expected attempts, odds and cost — with a calculator.

Key findings

  • Bolton passed 58.6% of tests and Bury 37.7% in April 2024–March 2025 — a 20.9-point gap.
  • The gap is statistically decisive: the 95% confidence ranges don't overlap.
  • At the 48.5% GB average you'd expect about 2.1 attempts; at Bury about 2.7.
  • Pass rates reflect roads and candidates, not harsher examiners — every examiner works to the same standard.
  • Being genuinely test-ready moves your odds far more than which centre you pick.

Pass-rate league tables are everywhere. What they don't tell you is what a pass rate actually means for you — how many attempts to expect, how much it could cost, and whether the gap between two test centres is real or just noise. We took the official DVSA counts for two Greater Manchester centres at opposite ends of the table and ran the numbers properly.

Bolton vs Bury: the same county, a 21-point gap

Is the gap real? With more than 5,500 tests at Bolton and 4,300 at Bury, the 95% confidence ranges (57.3–59.9% and 36.3–39.2%) don't come close to overlapping. A two-proportion test gives z ≈ 20.6 — a difference that size essentially never happens by chance. The centres really do produce different results.

Across Greater Manchester as a whole, independent site passrates.uk puts the 2024-25 average at around 50.1%, slightly above its 48.7% UK figure — but individual centres range from the high 30s to the high 50s and beyond.

What a pass rate means in attempts

A pass rate is passes per test, not per person. If you treat each attempt as having the same chance (a simplification — people usually improve after a fail), the maths is simple: expected attempts = 1 ÷ pass rate.

Why do centres differ so much?

A low pass rate doesn't mean examiners are harsher. The DVSA trains every examiner to the same standard. The differences come from the roads and the people:

  • Road layout. Centres with complex multi-lane roundabouts, bus lanes, tram crossings or fast dual carriageways nearby simply give candidates more chances to make a serious fault. Our test centre guides describe each one.
  • Who tests there. Centres serving lots of first-time candidates, people who booked before they were ready, or people re-testing quickly after a fail will have lower rates.
  • Traffic at test times. Congested routes create more hazards per mile — more decisions, more chances to hesitate or misjudge.
  • Readiness, above all. The biggest single factor is whether the candidate was genuinely test-ready. That's the one you control.

Should you travel to an "easier" centre?

Booking a higher-pass-rate centre across Greater Manchester is allowed — there are no catchment areas. But it often backfires: you'll be tested on roads you don't know, and your instructor may not have taught there. A learner who knows the Bury roads well is usually a safer bet at Bury than at a centre they've never driven around. Read our Bolton guide and our Bury guide for what each route is like.

What actually moves your odds: mock tests in test conditions, fixing every fault on your last report, and only booking when you can drive the full route without your instructor stepping in. The DriveSQ Student Portal's readiness checklist tracks exactly that.

How we worked this out

We used the test and pass counts for Bolton (3,250/5,548) and Bury (1,643/4,355) for April 2024–March 2025, as published from DVSA data by Mancunian Matters, and the 48.5% Great Britain average quoted alongside them. 95% confidence ranges use the Wilson score interval; the significance test is a pooled two-proportion z-test. "Expected attempts" and "chance within n tests" assume every attempt has the same pass probability (a geometric model) — real candidates usually improve between attempts, so treat these as a conservative baseline. Expected fees use the £62 weekday practical test fee. We only used centres where we could verify both counts.

Frequently asked questions

What is the driving test pass rate in Greater Manchester?
It varies by centre. DVSA figures for April 2024–March 2025 put Bolton at 58.6% and Bury at 37.7%, against a Great Britain average of 48.5%. Independent site passrates.uk estimates the Greater Manchester average at about 50.1% for 2024-25.
Which Greater Manchester test centre has the highest pass rate?
In the April 2024–March 2025 DVSA figures reported by Mancunian Matters, Bolton had the highest pass rate in Greater Manchester at 58.6% (3,250 passes from 5,548 tests).
How many attempts does it take to pass a driving test?
At the 48.5% Great Britain average, the expected number of attempts is about 2.1, and roughly 73% of people would pass within two tests if every attempt had the same chance. In practice most people improve after a fail.
Is the difference between test centres statistically significant?
Yes. Bolton's and Bury's 95% confidence ranges (57.3–59.9% and 36.3–39.2%) don't overlap, and a two-proportion z-test gives z ≈ 20.6, far beyond chance.
Should I book a test centre with a higher pass rate?
You can book any centre, but being tested on unfamiliar roads often cancels out any advantage. It's usually better to test where you've learned and practised.
