Becoming a driving instructor in Greater Manchester: the real route, costs and maths

Thinking about teaching people to drive in Greater Manchester? Most "become an instructor" pages are adverts for training courses. This one is the honest version: the official route, every DVSA fee, the bits that catch people out, and a calculator so you can model what you'd actually take home — with your real numbers, not ours.

The route to the green badge

To teach driving for money in Great Britain you must be a DVSA Approved Driving Instructor (ADI). You'll need to have held a full licence for at least three years, pass a background check, and then pass three tests:

  1. Part 1: theory and hazard perception £81

    100 multiple-choice questions in four bands — you need at least 85 overall and at least 20 in each band — plus a hazard perception test with a pass mark of 57 out of 75. Passing Part 1 starts a two-year clock to finish Parts 2 and 3.

  2. Part 2: your own driving £111

    An eyesight check and about an hour of driving to a much higher standard than the learner test: no more than six driving faults, and no serious or dangerous ones. You get three attempts.

  3. Optional: trainee licence £140

    After Part 2 and at least 40 hours of training, you can apply for a six-month "pink badge" to teach paying pupils while you prepare for Part 3 — a popular way to earn and build experience at the same time.

  4. Part 3: your teaching £111

    An examiner watches you give a real lesson for about an hour and marks you out of 51 across lesson planning, risk management and teaching and learning strategies. You need at least 31 overall and at least 8 for risk management; 43 or more is grade A. Again, three attempts.

  5. Register and qualify £300

    Pay the registration fee for your green ADI badge, valid for four years. From then on you'll take a periodic standards check to stay on the register.

The rule that catches people out: you must pass Parts 2 and 3 within two years of passing Part 1, with three attempts at each. Run out of attempts or time and you start again from Part 1.

Part 3 is where most people stall

Part 3 is the hardest step by a distance. Independent analysis of DVSA data puts the recent Part 3 pass rate at around 28% — far below the roughly 50% learner pass rate. The reason is simple: being a good driver and being a good teacher are different skills. The examiner wants to see you set goals with a real pupil, manage risk, adapt when things change and let the pupil do the learning.

The DVSA publishes quarterly pass rates for all three parts in its ADI qualifying test data — worth checking before you start, so you know what you're signing up for.

How we worked this out

The route, attempt limits and fees come from GOV.UK's "Become a driving instructor" guidance. The Part 3 pass rate is from passrates.uk's 2026 analysis of DVSA's ADI qualifying test data; the DVSA publishes the official quarterly figures (tables DRT511, DRT521) on GOV.UK. The DBS fee varies by provider, so we haven't quoted one. The earnings calculator is a simple model — lesson income (rate × paid hours × weeks) minus weekly car and franchise costs over 52 weeks and fuel per lesson hour. Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to become a driving instructor in the UK?
The DVSA fees are £81 for Part 1, £111 for Part 2, £111 for Part 3 and £300 to register — at least £603 for one attempt at each, plus a DBS check. The optional trainee licence is £140. Training and car costs are usually much larger than the fees.
How long do you have to pass the ADI tests?
You must pass Parts 2 and 3 within two years of passing Part 1, and you get three attempts at each of Parts 2 and 3. Otherwise you must start again from Part 1.
What is the ADI Part 3 pass rate?
Recent independent analysis of DVSA data puts the Part 3 pass rate at around 28% for 2025-26. The DVSA publishes official quarterly pass rates for all three parts.
Can I teach while I'm training to be an instructor?
Yes — after passing Part 2 and completing at least 40 hours of training you can apply for a six-month trainee licence (£140) and teach paying pupils while you prepare for Part 3.
How do I get pupils as a new driving instructor in Manchester?
Many new instructors join a school that supplies pupils. DriveSQ and SQ Driving School match learners to instructors by postcode and give instructors the DriveSQ Instructor Portal to manage them.
