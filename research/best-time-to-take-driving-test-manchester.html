Daylight & glare

The best time of year to take your driving test in Manchester

We calculated sunrise, sunset, dusk and low-sun glare for every month in Manchester. Here's what it means for your driving test — and when to book.

Key findings

  • The December sun never climbs above about 13° over Manchester — low enough to cause glare all day.
  • Commute-hour glare peaks in February (244 minutes a day), not in midwinter.
  • When the clocks go back on 25 October 2026, it gets dark about 64 minutes earlier overnight.
  • April–August gives the most daylight and least glare in test hours; March and September are a good balance.
  • The test standard never changes — practise the conditions of the month you'll test in.

Most advice about when to take your driving test is guesswork. So we did the maths. Using the same solar-position formulas the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) publishes, we calculated sunrise, sunset, dusk, the sun's height and — the part nobody talks about — the exact windows when a low sun sits in a driver's eyeline across Manchester, for every month of the year.

The result is a month-by-month picture of the conditions you'll actually drive in on test day. It doesn't change the test (the examiner marks the same skills in July and December), but it changes how hard those skills feel — and it should change how you practise.

Finding 1: December's sun never gets above 13°

On 15 December the sun peaks at just 13.3° above the horizon over Manchester. That's below our 15° "low sun" threshold all day long — so in December and much of January, dazzle isn't confined to rush hour. A sun that low sits directly behind traffic lights and on the far side of wet, reflective roads.

The shortest day, 21 December, gives you only 7h 29m of daylight (sunrise 08:23, sunset 15:52). The longest, 21 June, gives 17h 02m (sunrise 04:40, sunset 21:42). Same city, same test — more than double the daylight.

Finding 2: glare peaks in February, not midwinter

You might expect December to be the worst month for glare. It isn't — at least not in the hours most people drive. In February the low sun overlaps both the morning (07:35–09:37) and the afternoon (15:11–17:13) windows for a combined 244 minutes. In midwinter the sun rises later and sets before many people head home, so it spends less of the commute in your eyes.

Direction matters too. In December the sun rises in the south-east (about 130°) and sets in the south-west (about 230°); in June it rises far to the north-east (47°) and sets in the north-west (313°). So a winter-morning drive heading south-east — think the A6 towards Stockport, or the A34 towards Cheadle — puts the sun straight through the windscreen.

Finding 3: the clocks going back costs you 64 minutes overnight

On Saturday 24 October 2026 it's dark in Manchester from 18:29. On Monday 26 October it's dark from 17:25. If you've only ever practised in light evenings, the first week after the clocks change is a good time to book an extra lesson that finishes in the dark — headlight use, judging speed at junctions and spotting pedestrians in dark clothing all feel different.

British Summer Time starts again on 28 March 2027 and ends on 31 October 2027.

So when should you book?

There's no "easy" month — the examiner applies the same standard all year. But if you have a choice:

  • April to August gives the most daylight and the least glare during typical test hours. It's also when holiday demand is high, so book well ahead (see our waiting-times guide).
  • March and September are a sweet spot: 12–13 hours of daylight, short glare windows, and roads busy enough to be realistic.
  • October to February is perfectly passable — thousands of people pass every winter — but practise low sun, wet roads and dark evenings first, and pick a mid-morning slot if glare bothers you.
The honest answer: the best time to take your test is when you're consistently driving at test standard — not a particular month. That's exactly what the test-readiness checklist in the DriveSQ Student Portal is for.

How we worked this out

We implemented the NOAA solar-position algorithm (the same maths behind NOAA's online solar calculator) for Manchester city centre (53.4808°N, 2.2426°W) and ran it minute by minute for the 15th of every month, plus the solstices and the October 2026 clock change, applying UK daylight saving rules. Sunrise and sunset use the standard −0.833° sun-centre altitude (allowing for refraction and the sun's size); "dark" is the end of civil twilight (sun 6° below the horizon). We define low-sun glare as the sun being between 0.5° and 15° above the horizon during 07:00–10:00 or 15:00–19:00 — roughly when it's low enough to sit inside a driver's field of view. "Typical test hours" (08:00–16:00) are approximate. Times are accurate to about a minute.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best month to take a driving test in Manchester?
Based on our daylight and sun-position calculations, April to August offer the most daylight and the least low-sun glare in typical test hours, with March and September a good balance. The test standard is the same all year, so the best month is when you're consistently test-ready.
Is it harder to pass a driving test in winter?
The marking is identical, but conditions are tougher: in Manchester the shortest day has under 7½ hours of daylight and the December sun never rises above about 13°, so glare and dark, wet roads are common. Practise those conditions before your test.
When is sun glare worst for drivers in Manchester?
In our calculations, February has the most low-sun glare in the 07:00–10:00 and 15:00–19:00 windows, because the low sun overlaps both commutes. In midwinter the sun is low all day but spends less time in commute hours.
How much earlier does it get dark when the clocks go back?
In Manchester, dusk moves from 18:29 on 24 October 2026 to 17:25 on 26 October 2026 — about 64 minutes earlier overnight.
Do driving tests go ahead in the dark?
Yes. Tests run through the winter, and late-afternoon tests in December and January can finish around dusk. Driving tests are only cancelled for genuinely dangerous conditions such as snow or ice.
