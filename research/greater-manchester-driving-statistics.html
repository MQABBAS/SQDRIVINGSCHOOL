Driving in Greater Manchester by the numbers (2026)

Car ownership by borough, test-centre pass rates, daylight, costs and young driver safety — the key official numbers about driving in Greater Manchester, sourced.

Key findings

  • 27% of Greater Manchester households (319,000) have no car — above the 24% England average.
  • Car-free households range from 19% in Trafford to 39% in Manchester, and 68% in Piccadilly.
  • Test-centre pass rates range from 37.7% (Bury) to 58.6% (Bolton) for April 2024–March 2025.
  • Manchester gets 7h 29m of daylight on the shortest day and 17h 02m on the longest.
  • DVSA fees are £23 (theory) and £62/£75 (practical); SQ lessons are £35/hr, Student Portal free.

Car ownership: a city that still needs drivers

The 2021 Census shows Greater Manchester households are less likely to have a car than the national average — and the gap between boroughs is huge.

Inside the city of Manchester the contrast is sharper still: in Piccadilly 68% of households have no car, and in some central neighbourhoods more than three-quarters don't. Across Great Britain as a whole, 76.4% of households have at least one car or van.

Why it matters: a quarter of Greater Manchester households depend entirely on public transport, lifts or taxis. For jobs outside the city centre — warehouses, hospitals, shifts that start before the first tram — a licence can be the difference between applying and not. It's one reason we offer an NHS staff discount and a student discount.

How we worked this out

Car-ownership figures are from Census 2021 (ONS table TS045) as analysed by the Greater Manchester Combined Authority and Manchester City Council, with the Great Britain total from the RAC Foundation; Census 2021 took place during the pandemic, which may have affected some household patterns. Test-centre counts are DVSA figures for April 2024–March 2025 reported by Mancunian Matters. Daylight figures are our own NOAA-algorithm calculations. Young driver figures are from the DfT's younger driver factsheet; ADI figures are from GOV.UK and passrates.uk. Prices are SQ Driving School's published prices and the DVSA fees on GOV.UK.

Frequently asked questions

How many households in Greater Manchester don't have a car?
Census 2021 shows 27% of Greater Manchester households — about 319,000 households with 575,000 residents — have no car or van, compared with 24% across England.
Which Greater Manchester borough has the most car-free households?
Manchester, where 39% of households have no car. Trafford has the fewest at 19%. Within Manchester, 68% of households in Piccadilly have no car.
How much is the driving test in 2026?
The DVSA charges £23 for the theory test and £62 for a weekday practical test, or £75 for evenings, weekends and bank holidays.
How much are driving lessons with SQ Driving School?
£35 an hour for manual or automatic (2-hour lessons £70), a single 1-hour session £40, or 10 hours for £350 — £320 for NHS staff, students and M16, M18 and M19 postcodes. The DriveSQ Student Portal is free.
